# Kwik-Trip-Beer-Cave-Analysis
Overview

This project analyzes store-level sales and financial data from Kwik Trip to evaluate the true profitability of beer caves — walk-in coolers used to store and sell beer. The goal was to determine how overhead costs such as electricity, repairs, and asset depreciation affect gross profit and to assess whether beer caves lead to higher store and product-level profitability.

Key Objectives

Calculate "True Profit" by incorporating:

Electricity usage (33,080 kWh/year per cave)

Asset depreciation (straight-line over 7 years)

Repairs & maintenance costs from multiple sources

Compare profit performance of stores with vs. without beer caves

Identify top-selling and most profitable products by cave placement

Visualize product and store trends using ggplot2

Tools & Skills

R (tidyverse: dplyr, ggplot2, tidyr)

Data wrangling and joining multiple raw datasets

PDF text extraction for planogram UPC matching

Statistical visualization and business insight generation

Results

Stores with beer caves showed a higher median profit overall, despite more variability.

Products stored in beer caves generally had higher sales and profit.

A custom scatter plot highlighted product-level profit vs. sales, colored by cave placement.
